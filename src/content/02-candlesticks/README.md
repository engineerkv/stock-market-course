# Step 2: Candlesticks

A candle shows who won a single period: buyers, sellers, or neither. A candlestick pattern is one to three candles that together tell a short story, such as "sellers pushed hard, then buyers took over." This step teaches you to read those stories and, just as important, when to ignore them.

## The four placement rules

Learn these before any single pattern. They decide whether a pattern counts.

1. **A reversal pattern needs something to reverse.** A bullish reversal counts only after a fall and near support. A bearish reversal counts only after a rise and near resistance. The same shape in the middle of a range, or in the middle of a trend, is noise.
2. **Wait for the next candle.** Most patterns need confirmation: the next candle closes in the new direction, or price breaks the pattern's high or low.
3. **Bigger and longer means stronger.** A long wick shows a harder rejection than a short one. A large body shows more conviction than a small one. A pattern on a weekly chart beats the same pattern on a 5-minute chart.
4. **More candles and more volume mean more weight.** A three-candle pattern is usually more reliable than a two-candle pattern, which is more reliable than one candle. A pattern on above-average volume beats the same pattern on thin volume.

## Start with the core set

If you are new, learn these seven first: plain strong candle, hammer, shooting star, doji, engulfing, morning or evening star, and three white soldiers or three black crows. They cover reversal, rejection, indecision, and conviction. The other patterns add detail later.
