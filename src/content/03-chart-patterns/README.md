# Step 3: Chart Patterns

A chart pattern is a shape made by many candles over days, weeks, or months. Where a candle pattern shows one short fight, a chart pattern shows a whole battle. Because it takes longer to form, it usually leads to a bigger move, and most chart patterns give you a measured target.

Chart patterns fall into two groups.

- **Reversal patterns** form at the end of a trend and signal that it is turning. An uptrend turns down after a head and shoulders top, or a downtrend turns up after a double bottom.
- **Continuation patterns** form in the middle of a trend. They are a rest before the trend carries on, such as a flag or a triangle.

## Rules that apply to every chart pattern

1. **A reversal needs a trend to reverse.** A "double top" after a small bounce in a downtrend is not a double top.
2. **The pattern is not complete until price breaks the key line.** For a head and shoulders, that is the neckline. For a triangle, it is a trendline. Anticipating the break is guessing.
3. **Confirm with a close, and ideally with volume.** A close beyond the line on above-average volume is a strong break. A wick through the line that closes back inside is not a break.
4. **Expect a retest.** In large public studies of chart patterns, price comes back to test the broken line in most cases, often about two times out of three. A retest that holds is a second, often safer, entry.
5. **Targets are estimates.** Measured targets are reached a little over half the time for many patterns. Take partial profit near the target, and trail the rest.
6. **Taller and wider patterns tend to work better** than small, narrow ones, and patterns with the higher-timeframe trend behind them work better still.

## Start with the core set

Learn these first: head and shoulders (both kinds), double top and double bottom, bull and bear flag, and the three basic triangles. They appear often and have clear lines to trade.
